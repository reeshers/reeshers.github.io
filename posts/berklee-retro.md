It’s been about a month since I wrapped up my video production classes, and I figured I’d make my 2nd blog post reflecting on those 12 weeks of my life. 

## Why Berklee?

Back in January I decided to take a pause on the job search and enroll in classes that will help me get closer to my dream of having a career in media production. I wasn't really intersted in pursuing a whole new degree that would take years, so I looked into accelerated online programs that would take a couple months and still look impressive on a resume. 

It took some time to find a program that truly spoke to me, but after taking into account the curriculum, instructors, and cost I narrowed down my options to [NYU’s Certificate](https://www.sps.nyu.edu/certificates/tv-and-film-producing-accelerated.html) in TV and Film Producing and [Berklee’s Certificate](https://online.berklee.edu/certificates/video-production-professional) in Video Production. NYU is the more renowned film school, but I felt their program focused too much on the business aspects of film production. On the other hand, Berklee's program seemed to delve more into the technical skills and artistry of video production, but more so in the context of music videos. 

When all is said and done, I eventually want to become a filmmaker. What are music videos, if not short films? So I ended up choosing Berklee. 

## Starting the Semester

The program consisted of 3 classes: Digital Cinematography Fundamentals, Creative Video Editing and Music Video Production. Each class had weekly online lectures, readings and assignments. I haven't done any sort of schooling since graduating from UC Davis in 2019, plus I didn't have much of a music background to be enrolling into a college of music. My imposter syndrome was firing on all cylinders at the start of the semester. 

After initial introductions on the online forum, I learned that my fellow classmates came from all walks of life. Some were musicians, some were not, some were looking to transition careers like me, some were undergrads at Berklee, some were far into careers just looking to learn something new. So it didn't really matter what sort of background I had.

## Music Video Production

This class was easily the one I was most nervous about. Especially considering that our final project was to create a music video that required playback shots of a person lip-syncing or miming an instrument and storyline shots of a person doing some sort of action in front of the camera. I dreaded the idea of having to film myself, because I’ve always been uncomfortable looking at myself through the lens of a camera. The main reason being it forces me to face my dwarfism, an area of my life I've been struggling to have more ownership of. I easily could of asked my friends or family to act in my videos, but in order to grow as a person I decided to just embrace the discomfort. 

Using my Canon T8i, the cheapest tripod from my local Best Buy, a portable spotlight recommended by my professor, my little SF studio apartment, and myself as an actor, I was determined on making the music video on my own. The song I chose to use for my music video was [REEBOKS OR THE NIKES](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fbHxkNa7lhQ&list=RDfbHxkNa7lhQ) by [Jakey](https://www.youtube.com/@NakeyJakey), who is a YouTube content creator I’ve been following for a while. I always found his creativity and the fact he was able to create his full length album, ROMCOM, while consistently putting out YouTube content very inspiring. 

The overarching storyline I came up with for my music video was about someone who watches romantic comedy movies and feels inspired enough to write stories of their own. I used Bo Burnham’s [music video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Rx_p3NW7gQ&list=RD1Rx_p3NW7gQ) for All Eyes on Me as visual inspiration. You can watch the final video [here](https://vimeo.com/1180617305?fl=ip&fe=ec).

## Creative Video Editing

I hadn't touched Adobe Premiere Pro since high school, so it was a nice having a refresher on concepts like [keyframing](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/keyframing.html#:~:text=What%20is%20keyframing), [color correction](https://www.videomaker.com/how-to/editing/color-correction/everything-you-need-to-know-about-color-correction/) and [multi-cam editing](https://www.videomaker.com/article/12970-what-is-multicam-editing/). For my final project I wanted to create some kind of montage of San Francisco, since I am a big fan of urban photography and have been in love with the city since moving to the Bay Area in 2015. I aimed to make my visual love letter capture the city's hippie, wholesome vibes, but also the contrasting grungy, moody style the city can have when [Karl the Fog](https://www.sfgate.com/local/article/karl-the-fog-17752046.php#:~:text=Why%20is%20the%20fog%20in%20San%20Francisco%20called%20Karl) comes around. For music, I chose the aptly titled song [San Francisco](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j-447NaiBJs&list=RDj-447NaiBJs) by Scott McKenzie and a random find from my Spotify Discover Weekly, [goomba](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5idqNYoXvMs&list=RD5idqNYoXvMs) by oopsy, to add an element that felt more like me. 

Initially I wanted to film my own footage and capture different parts of the city. But frankly SF is a big city and I just didn't have the time or equipment to get the kind of footage that would be up to my satisfaction. So I decided to outsource through freelancers. Plus getting a chance to work with other creative people is always a blast. I made a post on Upwork and found a local videographer who seemed to understand my vision after discussing it with him online. He ended up providing me about 20 GB of footage, which was plenty for me to work with. 

Through lots of trial and error, I was able edit down the footage into a montage that was somewhat close to what I envisioned. You can see it [here](https://vimeo.com/1180617304?fl=ip&fe=ec). 

## Digital Cinematography Fundamentals

As a huge movie nerd I was looking forward to this class the most. I think what I appreciated most about the class was the instructor, [Nadine](https://www.nadinetruong.com/). Since she is an experienced filmmaker, she would occasionally use examples of her own work for teaching purposes which I found helpful and inspiring. Plus the feedback and words of encouragement she would provide on my projects were always appreciated. I definitely plan on keeping in touch with her to keep her around as a mentor figure as I pursue my career transition. 

Our weekly assignments would make us apply concepts like [camera angles](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/types-of-camera-shot-angles-in-film/), [camera movement](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/different-types-of-camera-movements-in-film/#:~:text=camera%20movement,-%3F), lighting and [continuity](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/hub/ideas/what-is-continuity-editing-in-film.html), so for my final project I decided to look back at the videos I made and create a sort of montage with added audio/music. Admittedly this project was a bit janky and a bit of an afterthought, but you can check it out [here](https://vimeo.com/1180907967?fl=ip&fe=ec). 

## That's a Wrap

Are my projects perfect? No. Will they win me any awards and be my claim to fame? Probably not. Are they mine and should I be proud of that? Definitely. 

I was hesitant of even posting my projects online, but I think it's important for me to share my work to build my confidence and portfolio. I have to constantly remind myself that everyone starts from somewhere and I shouldn't feel any shame in sharing my work. Not sure what the future has in store for me now but I look forward to it regardless. 

Note to future self: Let's aim to make a blog post once a month.
